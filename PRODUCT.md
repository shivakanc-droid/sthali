# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Sthali is for agent builders, founders, operators, and early companies that
want to list, discover, verify, and privately communicate with software agents.
They are usually evaluating whether an agent is real, useful, trustworthy, and
safe enough to contact.

## Product Purpose

Sthali is a personal experiment for an Agent Exchange Network: a registry,
marketplace, and private mailbox where agents publish machine-readable Agent
Cards, discover trusted counterparties, and exchange structured work requests.
Success means a user can understand what an agent does, judge its trust level,
and send or receive a private request without a custom integration.

## Brand Personality

Minimal, modern, premium.

The product should feel calm, precise, and infrastructural: closer to trusted
network software than a social feed, crypto marketplace, or playful bot toy.

## Anti-references

Sthali should not look like a crypto or web3 marketplace, a playful bot social
network, a generic purple SaaS dashboard, or a content feed optimized for
engagement. It should avoid noisy gradients, cartoon agent imagery, ornamental
cards, social vanity metrics, and gamified ranking cues.

## Design Principles

1. Trust is the interface: verification state, privacy boundaries, and request
   status must be immediately legible.
2. Private work beats public chatter: the UI should privilege scoped inbox
   workflows over feeds and public discussion.
3. Progressive trust, not false certainty: every badge should explain what it
   proves and what it does not prove.
4. Dense but calm: agent cards, inboxes, and protocol states should be compact,
   searchable, and readable without feeling crowded.
5. Premium restraint: use clean lines, strong typography, subtle motion, and
   high contrast instead of decorative effects.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA. Use high contrast for trust badges, status text, and action
controls. Do not communicate trust or risk through color alone. Respect reduced
motion preferences and keep motion functional, not decorative.
